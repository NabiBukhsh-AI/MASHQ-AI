# Welcoming customers at the branch

## Why the first minute matters

A customer decides how the visit will go in the first minute. A greeting, eye contact and a clear next step turn a queue into a conversation. Branch staff who greet every customer within thirty seconds receive fewer complaints and close requests faster, because the customer explains the need once and to the right person.

The branch standard is simple: greet, listen, confirm, act. Greet the customer by name when you know it. Listen without interrupting. Confirm what you heard in one sentence. Act, or hand over to the colleague who can act, and say who that is.

## Greeting

Stand or turn towards the customer, smile and say "Assalam o Alaikum" or "Good morning". Use "Sir" or "Madam" only until you know the name; after that, use the name with respect: "Mr Rasheed" or "Rasheed sahab". Elderly customers and customers with children are offered a seat before anything else.

Do not greet from behind a screen while typing. If you are finishing a task for another customer, look up, greet, and say how long it will take: "Two minutes, please, I will be right with you."

In Urdu, a warm opening is "Khush aamdeed, main aap ki kya madad kar sakta hoon?" A customer who answers in Urdu should hear Urdu back. A customer who mixes Urdu and English is comfortable with both; mirror them.

## Listening and confirming

Let the customer finish. Many complaints begin with a staff member guessing the request after three words. Note the key facts: the account type, the amount, the date and the document the customer has brought. Then confirm: "So you want to update the mobile number on your savings account, and you have your original CNIC with you. Correct?"

If the customer is upset, lower your voice and slow down. Say what you can do now and what will take time. Never say "that is not my department" without naming the person who will help and walking the customer to them.

## Identity first

Every account change starts with identity verification. Ask for the original CNIC. Compare the photograph and the signature with the form. A photocopy is not accepted for account changes. If the CNIC has expired, the account cannot be updated until it is renewed; direct the customer to NADRA and record the visit so the next colleague knows.

Never read a CNIC number aloud across the counter. Repeat only the last four digits if you must confirm it.

## Common requests and where they go

| Request                         | Who handles it                   | Typical time             |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Balance or statement            | Counter                          | 2 minutes                |
| Mobile number or address update | Counter with CNIC                | 5 minutes                |
| New account                     | Relationship officer             | 20 minutes               |
| Card block or reissue           | Counter, then card desk          | 5 minutes plus courier   |
| Complaint                       | Branch manager or complaint desk | Same day acknowledgement |

Tell the customer the typical time before you start. Customers tolerate waiting when they know how long it will be.

## Handing over

When you hand a customer to a colleague, walk with them or point clearly, give the colleague the summary in one sentence, and say goodbye to the customer by name. The customer should never have to explain the request twice.

## Closing the visit

Before the customer leaves, confirm the outcome: what was done, what happens next and when. Offer a printed or SMS confirmation where the system provides one. Thank the customer and invite them back: "Shukriya, phir tashreef laiye."

## Common mistakes

1. Greeting late or not at all while finishing paperwork.
2. Asking for the CNIC before greeting the customer.
3. Reading account numbers or CNIC numbers aloud.
4. Sending the customer to another counter without an introduction.
5. Promising a time you cannot keep. Say "usually five minutes" rather than "one minute".

## Quick practice

A customer in his seventies arrives at 12:40 looking confused, holding a folded statement. What do you do first? Offer a seat, greet him warmly, and ask an open question: "Aap kis silsile mein tashreef laye hain?" Then listen.

A young customer says in one breath: "I need to change my number, block my card and get a statement." Confirm all three, tell her the order and the time, and start with the card block because it protects her money.

# Account opening and know your customer (KYC)

## Purpose

This guide covers the steps a branch officer follows to open a personal current or savings account, the documents required, and the checks that protect the customer and the bank. It applies to walk-in customers at any branch. Business accounts follow a separate procedure.

## Step 1: Understand the need

Ask what the customer wants the account for: salary, savings, remittances or daily payments. The answer decides the product. A salaried customer who wants a debit card and mobile banking usually needs a current account. A customer saving for a goal needs a savings account, which pays profit monthly and has a minimum balance of five thousand rupees.

Explain the fees before the form: the debit card fee, the SMS alert fee and any minimum balance charge. A customer who learns about fees from the first statement will not trust the branch again.

## Step 2: Verify identity

Ask for the original computerized national identity card (CNIC). A photocopy, a photo on a phone or an expired card is not accepted. Check three things: the photograph matches the person, the card has not expired, and the name matches the form exactly, including spelling.

Run the NADRA verification in the system. The verification returns a match within seconds; if it fails twice, stop and involve the branch operations manager. Do not proceed on a third attempt without approval.

For customers who cannot sign, record a thumb impression in the presence of the branch operations manager and note it on the form.

## Step 3: Complete the KYC form

The KYC form records the source of income, the expected monthly turnover, the purpose of the account and whether the customer is a politically exposed person. Fill it with the customer, not for the customer. Read each answer back.

Typical turnover for a salaried customer equals about one to two months of salary. A declared turnover that is ten times the salary needs an explanation and the manager's sign-off. Record the explanation in the notes field, not on a sticky note.

## Step 4: Collect supporting documents

| Customer type      | Documents                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Salaried           | CNIC, salary slip or employer letter                                                      |
| Self-employed      | CNIC, proof of business such as a tax registration or a utility bill in the business name |
| Student            | CNIC, student card, and the guardian's CNIC for customers under eighteen                  |
| Pensioner          | CNIC and the pension book or letter                                                       |
| Overseas Pakistani | CNIC or NICOP and proof of overseas income                                                |

Scan each document into the system, return the originals, and confirm on the checklist that every scan is readable.

## Step 5: Capture signatures and biometrics

Take the specimen signature on the card and in the system. The customer signs the same way every time; if the two signatures differ, ask for a third and keep the closest two. Capture the biometric thumb impression when the device is available.

## Step 6: Activate and hand over

Once the account is approved, give the customer the account number in writing, explain how to register for mobile banking, and tell them when the debit card will arrive (usually seven working days by courier to the registered address). Send the welcome SMS from the system.

Confirm the registered mobile number by reading back only the last four digits. The customer must own the number; a family member's number is not accepted because one-time passwords go to it.

## What can go wrong

- The customer's CNIC address differs from the current address. Accept a utility bill or a rental agreement as proof of the current address and record both.
- The customer wants to open the account for someone else. Every personal account is opened by the account holder in person. Explain politely and offer to book a time.
- The customer refuses to declare the source of income. The account cannot be opened. Explain that the declaration is a regulatory requirement, not a bank preference.
- The system verification is down. Do not open the account on paper. Take the customer's details, give a reference number and call back within one working day.

## Timing

A complete opening takes about twenty minutes when the customer has every document. Tell the customer this at the start. Missing documents add a second visit; offer a checklist on the spot so the second visit succeeds.

## Practice questions

Why do we verify identity before completing the KYC form? Because every later step depends on knowing who the customer is; a form completed for the wrong person cannot be corrected.

A customer declares a turnover of two million rupees a month on a salary of eighty thousand. What do you do? Ask for the reason, record it, and get the manager's sign-off before opening the account.
